More Than a Whistle and a Flag

Football officiating is a team sport.

Every official has specific responsibilities, but the crew has one common goal: work together to manage the game fairly, safely, and professionally. As an official, you'll develop skills that go far beyond knowing the rules.

  • Be Part of a Crew

    Work alongside other officials who prepare, communicate, and make decisions together.

  • Stay Close to Football

    Continue being part of the game even when you're no longer playing or coaching.

  • Challenge Yourself

    Develop your rules knowledge, positioning, communication, concentration, and judgment.

  • Keep Improving

    Every meeting, practice, and game gives you another opportunity to get better.

Never Officiated Before?

That's what training is for.

You don't need to walk onto the field already knowing everything. GNOA helps new football officials learn the fundamentals before they're expected to handle them in a game.

You'll learn:

  • Basic rules and responsibilities
  • Where to stand and what to watch
  • Officiating mechanics and signals
  • Crew communication
  • Pre-game preparation
  • Player and coach communication
  • Game management
  • How to learn from every assignment

You will learn alongside officials who have already worked the games, handled difficult situations, and developed their craft.

Start the Process

What Does a Football Official Learn?

  • Rules

    Knowing the rules is the foundation. Training helps you understand both the rule itself and how it applies during a live game.

  • Positioning

    Different officials see different parts of the play. Learn where to be, where to look, and how your position fits into the entire crew.

  • Mechanics

    Movement, signals, coverage, timing, and communication create consistency across the officiating crew.

  • Judgment

    Not every decision comes from memorizing a rulebook. Good officials learn to recognize what matters and apply the rules to real situations.

  • Communication

    Officials communicate constantly, with their crew, players, coaches, and game administrators.

Your First Game Is Just the Beginning

Your first assignment will probably feel fast.

That's normal.

The goal isn't to prove that you already know everything.

It's to apply what you've learned, work with your crew, ask questions, receive feedback, and be a little better the next time you step onto the field.

One game becomes ten.

Ten games become a season.

And what once seemed overwhelming starts becoming familiar.

Football News & Recognition

What Will I Need?

Don't buy a full officiating kit before talking with GNOA.

Football has specific uniform and equipment requirements, and we'll help you understand what you actually need before you spend money.

Depending on your position and assignment, equipment may include your approved uniform, whistle, penalty flag, down indicator, game cards, timing equipment, and other officiating tools.

Talk to us first. Buy second.

Football Officiating FAQs

Do I need previous officiating experience?

No. GNOA trains and develops new officials.

Do I have to have played football?

No. Playing or coaching experience can help with familiarity, but officiating is its own skill and can be learned.

Will I start with varsity games?

Officials develop over time. Assignments should be appropriate for your training, experience, and readiness.

Do officials work alone?

No. Football officiating is built around the crew. Each official has different responsibilities, and everyone works together.

Is football officiating physically demanding?

Officials need to move with the game and maintain proper positioning. The demands vary by position and assignment.

When should I buy my uniform and equipment?

Talk with GNOA first so you understand the current requirements before purchasing anything.
