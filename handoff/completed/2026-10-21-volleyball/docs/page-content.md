## What Makes a Good Volleyball Official?

### Focus
Volleyball demands concentration.

The ball moves quickly, and officials need to stay engaged from the serve through the end of every rally.

### Positioning
Good positioning helps officials see the action clearly and make better decisions.

### Rules Knowledge
Rotations, substitutions, player position, contact, and net play all require a solid understanding of the rules.

### Communication
Signals need to be clear and consistent.

Officials also communicate with partners, coaches, players, and the scorer’s table throughout the match.

### Composure
Matches can become intense.

Good officials stay calm, professional, and focused on the next rally.

## Volleyball Officiating FAQs

### Do I need previous officiating experience?
No. GNOA trains and develops new volleyball officials.

### Do I need to have played volleyball?
No. Playing or coaching experience can help with familiarity, but officiating is its own skill.

### Will I start with varsity matches?
New officials develop over time. Assignments should match your training, experience, and readiness.

### Is volleyball officiating physically demanding?
It is generally less running-intensive than some sports, but it requires strong concentration, positioning, and sustained focus throughout the match.

### Do volleyball officials work alone?
No. Volleyball uses an officiating team, with responsibilities shared between officials and table personnel.

### Should I buy equipment now?
Talk with GNOA first. We can help you understand the current requirements and whether used or hand-me-down equipment may be available.
