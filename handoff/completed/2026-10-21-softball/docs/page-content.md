# Become a Softball Umpire in Southwest Florida

**Stay in the game. See every play differently.**

Softball umpiring puts you close to the action and challenges you to make confident decisions in a game that can change quickly.

You’ll learn positioning, mechanics, rules, timing, communication, and how to work with your crew to manage the game.

Greater Naples Officials Association recruits, trains, and develops new and experienced softball umpires throughout Naples, Collier County, and Southwest Florida.

## Fast Game. Good Position. Strong Judgment.

A pitch can become a bunt, steal, force play, tag play, or play at the plate in seconds. Good umpires learn where to be, what to watch, and when to make the call.

### Stay Close to Softball
Continue being part of the sport you enjoy.

### Learn the Craft
Rules, positioning, timing, mechanics, communication, and judgment.

### Work as a Crew
Plate and base umpires work together to cover the field.

### Challenge Yourself
Every game brings new situations and opportunities to improve.

## Never Umpired Before?
### That’s what training is for.

You don’t need previous umpiring experience. GNOA helps new softball umpires learn the fundamentals and build confidence before taking on more challenging assignments.

**You’ll learn:**
- Rules and responsibilities
- Plate and base positioning
- Mechanics and signals
- Strike-zone fundamentals
- Fair and foul responsibilities
- Crew communication
- Game management
- Working with players and coaches
- How to improve through feedback

**Supporting callout:** You won’t learn everything at once. You build it one game at a time.

## What Makes a Good Umpire?
### It’s a combination of skills.

### Positioning
Get the right angle and distance to see the entire play.

### Timing
See the play finish before making the call.

### Rules Knowledge
Understand the rules and how to apply them when the unexpected happens.

### Communication
Work with partners, coaches, players, and game administrators.

### Composure
Stay calm, communicate professionally, and focus on the next pitch.
