## What Does a Baseball Umpire Learn?
### Positioning
Being in the right place gives you the best opportunity to make the right call. Learn how positioning changes based on runners, outs, crew size, and the developing play.

### Plate Work
Calling balls and strikes is one of the most visible parts of umpiring. You’ll learn stance, positioning, timing, tracking the pitch, signals, and how to develop consistency behind the plate.

### Base Mechanics
Good base umpiring requires positioning, angle, timing, concentration, and understanding where the next play may happen.

### Rules
Training helps you understand the rules and, more importantly, how to apply them when something unusual happens.

### Judgment
Great umpiring is about seeing the entire play, processing what happened, and making a confident decision.

### Communication
Umpires communicate with their partners, coaches, players, and game administrators throughout the game.

## Timing Matters
One of the first things new umpires learn is that you don’t have to rush. See the play. Process what happened. Then make the call. Confidence comes from good positioning, proper mechanics, preparation, and experience—not from trying to be the loudest person on the field. Those skills develop one game at a time.

## Your First Game Is Just the Beginning
Your first game may feel like everything is happening at once. That’s normal. With experience, you begin to understand where to look, what matters, where you need to move, and how your responsibilities fit with the rest of the crew. One game becomes ten. Ten games become a season. And gradually, the game begins to slow down.

## What Will I Need?
Don’t buy everything before talking with GNOA. Baseball umpires need specific uniforms and equipment, particularly for work behind the plate, but getting started does not necessarily mean buying an entire set of brand-new gear.

Depending on your assignments, equipment may include approved umpire uniform, umpire shoes, ball bags, indicator, plate brush, protective mask, chest protector, shin guards, and other protective equipment.

Experienced umpires often have extra equipment, older gear that is still in good condition, or items they are willing to pass along to someone just getting started. Hand-me-down, borrowed, or used equipment may sometimes be available while you are learning what you need.

**Talk to us first. See what’s available. Buy what you need.**

## Baseball Umpiring FAQs
- Do I need previous umpiring experience? — No. GNOA helps train and develop new baseball umpires.
- Do I need to have played baseball? — No. Playing or coaching experience can give you familiarity with the game, but umpiring is its own skill.
- Do I have to start behind the plate? — GNOA baseball leadership can explain how new officials typically progress.
- Is calling balls and strikes difficult? — It is a skill that develops through training and repetition.
- Do baseball umpires work alone? — Most games involve an umpiring crew.
- Do I have to know every rule before my first game? — No. You need a solid foundation, but rules knowledge continues developing throughout an umpire’s career.
- Should I buy umpire equipment now? — Talk with GNOA first.
